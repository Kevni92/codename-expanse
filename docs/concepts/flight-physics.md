## Purpose

Flight Physics defines the authoritative physical behaviour of ships and other dynamic objects in Codename Expanse. It establishes the inertial movement model, thrust, rotation, gravity, collisions, structural loading and the distinction between dynamic objects and prescribed world objects.

The system is intentionally physically grounded without attempting to simulate every real-world effect. It should provide a coherent Newtonian foundation that supports manual flight, combat, long-distance travel and later autopilot trajectory planning.

## Design Goals

- **G-FP-001:** Preserve true inertial movement: removing thrust must not implicitly remove velocity or angular velocity.
- **G-FP-002:** Make ship mass and rotational inertia materially affect handling and ship-class identity.
- **G-FP-003:** Keep the physical model coherent enough that manual flight and autopilot use the same underlying rules.
- **G-FP-004:** Support real gravitational trajectories around moving celestial bodies without requiring a full N-body simulation.
- **G-FP-005:** Keep physical complexity only where it creates meaningful gameplay or systemic consistency.
- **G-FP-006:** Apply the same authoritative flight rules to player ships and NPC ships.
- **G-FP-007:** Permit physically extreme behaviour, including very high speeds and rotation, while allowing structural integrity to provide the practical physical limit rather than arbitrary caps.

## Scope

This concept owns:

- linear inertial movement;
- rotational inertial movement;
- thrust-to-acceleration behaviour;
- ship mass and abstract rotational inertia;
- gravity affecting dynamic objects;
- collision response between dynamic objects;
- structural load produced by acceleration and rotation;
- physical inheritance of motion by projectiles and detached debris;
- prescribed movement versus dynamic movement;
- the global inertial reference frame used by gameplay physics.

## Out of Scope

This concept does not define:

- Flight Assist behaviour, damping or control automation;
- autopilot trajectory-planning algorithms or player interaction;
- ship construction and hardpoint rules;
- exact module damage or structural-failure resolution;
- weapon damage, projectile lifetime or weapon-specific behaviour;
- station gameplay and station interaction;
- HUD layout or navigation UI;
- technical simulation timestep, numerical integration algorithm or data structures.

These systems may consume Flight Physics rules but must not redefine them silently.

## Terminology

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **Dynamic object** | A gameplay object whose position, velocity and rotation are advanced by the physical simulation. Ships, projectiles, interactive asteroids and debris are dynamic objects. |
| **Prescribed object** | An object whose motion follows an authored/generated trajectory rather than responding dynamically to forces. Celestial bodies and stations are prescribed objects. |
| **Celestial body** | A star, planet, moon or comparable world-scale body. Celestial bodies may move on prescribed orbits and are the only gravitational sources. |
| **Linear velocity** | The velocity vector of a dynamic object in the global inertial reference frame. |
| **Angular velocity** | The rotational rate of a dynamic object. It persists without counter-torque. |
| **Thrust** | Force generated by propulsion or maneuvering systems. |
| **Rotational inertia** | The abstract resistance of an object to changes in angular velocity. It depends on ship scale/mass but does not simulate exact module mass distribution. |
| **Structural load** | Physical stress resulting from strong linear acceleration, angular acceleration, angular velocity or collisions. |
| **Gravity softening** | The per-celestial-body smoothing of gravitational acceleration near its center to avoid a singularity. |

## Player Experience

A ship should feel like a mass moving through space rather than a vehicle moving through air. If the player stops thrusting, the ship continues on its current trajectory. If the ship is rotating and no counter-torque is applied, it continues rotating.

Large or heavily loaded ships should feel materially more inertial than light ships. Strong forward propulsion gives each ship a clear orientation, while weaker reverse and lateral thrust allow meaningful maneuvering without making facing irrelevant.

The physical model should allow skilled players to exploit inertia, gravity and relative motion manually. Less technical players are expected to rely on later Flight Assist and Autopilot systems that operate within the same physical rules rather than bypassing them.

## Functional Design

### Linear movement

Dynamic objects follow Newtonian inertial movement. Linear velocity persists unless an external force changes it.

Ship propulsion generates force rather than directly setting speed or acceleration. Current total mass therefore affects acceleration: increasing mass while available thrust remains unchanged reduces acceleration.

Normal propulsion has no gameplay fuel or reaction-mass consumption in this concept.

There is no artificial maximum linear speed. The simulation uses classical Newtonian mechanics and does not introduce relativistic effects or a light-speed limit.

### Ship mass

A ship's current mass includes its hull/base mass plus installed modules and carried cargo where applicable. Changes to loadout or cargo can therefore change linear acceleration.

Additional mass also increases rotational inertia, but the exact spatial distribution of that mass is not simulated.

### Propulsion directions

Ships have a dominant forward main thrust capability. Reverse and lateral maneuvering thrust exists but is weaker than forward thrust.

Propulsion reacts immediately to control demand at the Flight Physics level; engine spool-up and similar behaviour are not part of the base model unless later defined for a specific propulsion system.

Main thrust is abstracted as acting correctly through the center of mass. It does not create unintended rotation from load imbalance.

### Rotation

Rotation is inertial. Applying rotational control changes angular velocity through torque; removing torque does not automatically stop rotation.

Rotational inertia depends on ship scale and mass. Exact module placement does not alter center of mass or rotational inertia distribution.

There is no artificial maximum angular velocity. Instead, sufficiently high angular velocity or angular acceleration may exceed structural limits and create damage or structural failure through the later damage/structure system.

### Gravity

Only celestial bodies generate gravity.

Gravity acts on dynamic objects including ships, projectiles, interactive asteroids and debris. Dynamic objects do not generate gravity themselves.

Celestial bodies do not gravitationally affect one another. Their positions follow prescribed orbits/trajectories. The current simulated position of a celestial body is nevertheless the origin of its gravitational field.

Gravitational acceleration is independent of the mass of the affected dynamic object. Multiple celestial-body gravitational influences are combined simultaneously.

Gravity has no hard influence radius. Its effect decreases continuously with distance.

Each celestial body has its own gravity-softening scale. Near the body's center, the field is smoothed so acceleration remains finite.

Celestial bodies are not collision geometry. Their visible representation may occupy a deeper parallax layer, so a dynamic object may cross the projected visual footprint without physically colliding with the body.

### Prescribed celestial motion

Planets, moons and other celestial bodies may orbit through authored or procedurally defined motion. Those orbits are not generated by mutual gravitational simulation.

This preserves a moving star system while avoiding N-body instability and unnecessary systemic complexity.

### Stations

Stations follow prescribed trajectories, such as a defined orbit around a celestial body. They are not dynamically held in orbit by gravity.

Stations have no physical collision response under this concept. Dynamic objects may cross their projected representation without impulse exchange or collision damage.

### Dynamic asteroids

Interactive asteroids are dynamic objects. They respond to celestial gravity, participate in dynamic-object collisions and may have their trajectories changed by physical interactions.

### Dynamic-object collisions

Collisions between dynamic objects exchange momentum according to their masses and relative motion.

Off-center impacts may generate angular motion in addition to changing linear velocity.

Collisions are not perfectly elastic. Objects may rebound, but some kinetic energy may be dissipated through deformation and damage. Exact material response and damage calculation belong to later concepts/architecture.

Collision severity depends primarily on relative velocity and participating mass rather than a fixed per-object collision-damage value.

### Projectile inheritance

When a projectile is created, it inherits the current linear velocity of the firing object and adds its own launch/muzzle velocity in the relevant firing direction.

Weapons do not impart recoil to the firing ship. This is an intentional gameplay abstraction.

Once created, a projectile is a dynamic object and can be affected by celestial gravity where applicable.

### Debris inheritance

Detached ship parts and debris become dynamic objects. They inherit the parent object's current movement and rotation at the moment of separation, plus any additional impulse caused by the separation event.

### Structural loading

Ships have ship-specific structural limits.

The Flight Physics layer exposes physical loading from:

- strong linear acceleration;
- strong angular acceleration;
- high angular velocity;
- collisions.

Exceeding a structural limit has an immediate, graduated effect: a larger exceedance represents more severe loading. The later ship/damage system owns the conversion of load into module damage, hull damage, fragmentation or total structural failure.

Crew biological G-force limits are not simulated by this concept.

### Shared player and NPC physics

Player-controlled and NPC-controlled ships use the same authoritative physical rules. AI may choose different control strategies but must not receive a separate movement model.

### Reference frame

The authoritative physical simulation uses one global inertial reference frame for the star system.

Local relative frames may later be used for UI, navigation and autopilot reasoning, but they do not replace or alter the authoritative physical state.

## Rules and Invariants

1. **C-FP-001:** A dynamic object's linear velocity persists unless changed by external force or collision.
2. **C-FP-002:** A dynamic object's angular velocity persists unless changed by torque or collision.
3. **C-FP-003:** Ship acceleration results from applied thrust relative to current ship mass; thrust does not directly set velocity.
4. **C-FP-004:** A ship's total mass includes gameplay-relevant installed modules and cargo.
5. **C-FP-005:** Additional ship mass reduces acceleration for unchanged thrust and increases abstract rotational inertia.
6. **C-FP-006:** Main forward thrust is stronger than reverse and lateral maneuvering thrust.
7. **C-FP-007:** Main thrust is abstracted to act through the ship's center of mass and does not produce accidental torque from load distribution.
8. **C-FP-008:** There is no artificial maximum linear speed.
9. **C-FP-009:** There is no artificial maximum angular velocity.
10. **C-FP-010:** Classical Newtonian mechanics is used; relativistic effects and a light-speed cap are not simulated.
11. **C-FP-011:** Normal propulsion does not consume a gameplay fuel/reaction-mass resource.
12. **C-FP-012:** Propulsion force responds immediately at the base Flight Physics level.
13. **C-FP-013:** Only celestial bodies generate gravitational acceleration.
14. **C-FP-014:** Dynamic objects do not gravitationally attract one another.
15. **C-FP-015:** Celestial bodies do not gravitationally affect one another.
16. **C-FP-016:** Gravity from all relevant celestial bodies is combined for each affected dynamic object.
17. **C-FP-017:** Gravity has no hard range cutoff.
18. **C-FP-018:** Gravitational acceleration is independent of the affected object's mass.
19. **C-FP-019:** Each celestial body uses a body-specific gravity-softening scale near its center.
20. **C-FP-020:** Celestial bodies move on prescribed trajectories and are not dynamic collision bodies.
21. **C-FP-021:** Stations move on prescribed trajectories and are not physical collision bodies.
22. **C-FP-022:** Interactive asteroids are dynamic objects affected by celestial gravity and dynamic-object collisions.
23. **C-FP-023:** Dynamic-object collisions conserve momentum at the gameplay-physics level while allowing energy loss through inelastic response/damage.
24. **C-FP-024:** Off-center dynamic-object collisions may alter both linear and angular motion.
25. **C-FP-025:** Collision severity depends on relative motion and mass rather than a fixed collision-damage value.
26. **C-FP-026:** Projectiles inherit the firing object's linear velocity and add their own launch velocity.
27. **C-FP-027:** Weapon firing does not produce recoil on the firing ship.
28. **C-FP-028:** Detached debris inherits the parent object's movement and rotation plus any separation impulse.
29. **C-FP-029:** Ships have ship-specific structural load limits.
30. **C-FP-030:** Strong linear acceleration, angular acceleration, angular velocity and collisions may generate structural overload.
31. **C-FP-031:** Structural overload is immediate and graduated rather than a simple delayed binary threshold.
32. **C-FP-032:** Structural overload may ultimately cause fragmentation or total structural failure, resolved by the owning damage/structure system.
33. **C-FP-033:** Crew biological G-force limits are not part of Flight Physics.
34. **C-FP-034:** Player and NPC ships use the same physical movement rules.
35. **C-FP-035:** Authoritative physical state is expressed in a single global inertial reference frame.

## States and Transitions

Flight Physics itself has no discrete gameplay state machine. Dynamic objects continuously evolve according to force, momentum, torque, gravity and collision interactions.

Objects may transition between higher-level ownership states, for example an attached ship component becoming detached debris, but those transition triggers belong to the owning damage/module system. Once detached, the resulting object follows the dynamic-object rules defined here.

## Interactions With Other Systems

- [Game Vision](../vision/game-vision.md) — establishes inertial flight, visible ships/modules, long-range projectiles and physically grounded simulation as product direction.
- Flight Assist concept (future) — may generate thrust/torque inputs to stabilize or simplify control but must not replace the physical model.
- Autopilot concept (future) — plans physically valid trajectories under the forces and thrust capabilities defined here.
- Ships/Modules concept (future) — owns ship mass, thrust capabilities, installed modules and ship-specific structural limits.
- Damage/Structure concept (future) — owns conversion of structural load and collision severity into damage and fragmentation.
- Weapons/Projectiles concept (future) — owns projectile-specific launch speed, damage and lifetime while inheriting movement rules from this concept.
- World/Orbit concept (future) — owns prescribed celestial and station trajectories while Flight Physics consumes their current position for gravity/reference calculations.
- Rendering/Parallax concept (future) — owns the deeper visual presentation of celestial bodies; visual footprint does not define collision geometry.

## Player Feedback and Information

Flight Physics requires later UI/HUD systems to expose enough information that the player can understand important physical consequences. At minimum, future interfaces should be capable of communicating:

- current linear velocity and relative velocity when relevant;
- current rotational motion when relevant to control;
- current acceleration/thrust state;
- structural-overload warnings before or while dangerous loading occurs;
- relevant gravitational influence/navigation context where useful;
- collision or impact consequences;
- physical limits that prevent a requested assist/autopilot maneuver from being executed.

Detailed HUD layout is outside this concept.

## Edge Cases

### Passing through the projected area of a celestial body

No collision occurs. The body's rendered footprint may be on a deeper parallax layer and does not represent same-plane collision geometry. Gravity continues to be evaluated from the body's simulated position.

### Passing very near a celestial body's center

Gravity softening prevents acceleration from becoming singular or unbounded. The dynamic object remains physically simulated and may pass through the body's projected center because celestial collision geometry does not exist.

### Multiple overlapping gravity fields

All applicable celestial-body gravitational accelerations combine. No single "dominant body" replaces the others.

### Extremely high speed

No artificial speed cap is applied, even if classical Newtonian gameplay permits speeds that would be relativistically impossible in reality. This is an intentional abstraction.

### Extreme rotation

No artificial angular-speed cap is applied. Structural loading may instead exceed the ship's limits and later cause damage or fragmentation.

### Damaged propulsion

Flight Physics accepts the currently available thrust/torque capability from the owning ship/module system. Damage may therefore reduce physical control authority, but the damage model itself is external.

### Collision with celestial bodies or stations

No physical collision occurs because neither is collision geometry under this concept.

### Collision between dynamic objects

Momentum exchange and possible angular effects occur even if one object is much more massive than the other. Exact numerical collision response belongs to technical architecture so long as the observable result remains consistent with the rules here.

## Examples

### Added cargo reduces acceleration

A ship using the same engine thrust accelerates more slowly after taking on heavy cargo because current total mass has increased.

### Inertial drift

A ship accelerates forward, stops thrusting and rotates 90 degrees. Its previous linear velocity remains unchanged until new thrust alters it. Facing direction alone does not redirect existing velocity.

### Rotational inertia

A rotating ship continues rotating after rotational thrust is released. A Flight Assist may later apply counter-torque automatically, but the underlying physical rule remains inertial.

### Projectile inheritance

A ship moving rapidly through the global reference frame fires a projectile forward. The projectile begins with the ship's current velocity plus its own muzzle velocity. Firing does not slow the ship through recoil.

### Orbital navigation foundation

A station may follow a prescribed orbit around a planet. The planet's current position produces gravity that affects a player ship. A later autopilot may plan a thrust trajectory that arrives at the station with appropriate relative position and velocity, but the station itself does not require dynamic gravity simulation to remain in orbit.

## Design Decisions and Rationale

| ID | Decision | Rationale | Consequences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D-FP-001 | Use fully inertial linear and rotational movement. | Creates the physically grounded core experience and supports meaningful assists rather than fake physics. | Manual flight can be demanding; assists become important accessibility/control systems. |
| D-FP-002 | Use force and mass rather than direct acceleration values as the conceptual propulsion model. | Gives ship mass, modules and cargo systemic meaning. | Loadout affects handling. |
| D-FP-003 | Use strong forward thrust and weaker reverse/lateral thrust. | Preserves ship facing and maneuvering decisions. | Orientation matters even with strafing capability. |
| D-FP-004 | Do not impose artificial linear or angular speed caps. | Avoids hidden nonphysical constraints and preserves simulation freedom. | Structural limits and navigation/control systems must handle extreme states. |
| D-FP-005 | Use classical Newtonian rather than relativistic mechanics. | Relativity adds major complexity without proportional gameplay value. | Speeds may mathematically exceed real-world light speed. |
| D-FP-006 | Only celestial bodies generate gravity. | Keeps meaningful orbital/gravity gameplay while avoiding unnecessary many-body simulation. | Ships, asteroids and projectiles do not attract one another. |
| D-FP-007 | Celestial bodies and stations follow prescribed motion. | Keeps a stable moving world without requiring N-body maintenance or dynamic station orbit correction. | Their trajectories are world-content rules rather than emergent force results. |
| D-FP-008 | Celestial bodies and stations are non-collidable. | Their deeper parallax presentation means projected overlap need not imply same-plane physical contact. | Players can cross their projected footprints; collision gameplay focuses on dynamic objects. |
| D-FP-009 | Use per-body gravity softening. | Prevents singular behaviour while retaining continuous long-range gravity. | Near-center gravity is intentionally not a literal point-mass solution. |
| D-FP-010 | Dynamic-object collisions exchange momentum and can induce rotation. | Keeps collisions consistent with the inertial physical model. | Collision outcomes depend on mass, relative velocity and impact geometry. |
| D-FP-011 | Weapons have no recoil. | Avoids making weapon firing destabilize ship control while preserving projectile inertia. | This is a deliberate exception to strict momentum conservation across weapon firing. |
| D-FP-012 | Structural integrity, not arbitrary motion caps, limits extreme acceleration/rotation. | Converts extreme physics into gameplay risk instead of invisible clamps. | Requires later structure/damage rules. |
| D-FP-013 | Do not simulate crew G-force limits. | Avoids importing a crew-survival simulation into the core flight model. | Ship structure is the relevant physical-load constraint for now. |
| D-FP-014 | Player and NPC ships use identical physics. | Preserves systemic trust and prevents visibly cheating movement. | AI must operate within real ship capabilities. |
| D-FP-015 | Use a single global inertial reference frame. | Provides one authoritative interpretation of velocity and momentum. | Local frames may exist only as derived navigation/UI constructs. |

## Rejected or Deferred Alternatives

| Alternative | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Elite Dangerous-style separate simplified cruise physics | Rejected | The intended direction is one physically grounded simulation with later autopilot trajectory planning rather than a separate travel-physics mode. |
| Artificial maximum ship speed | Rejected | Conflicts with the chosen inertial simulation freedom. |
| Artificial maximum angular speed | Rejected | Structural integrity is intended to provide the meaningful physical limit instead. |
| Full N-body gravity between celestial bodies | Rejected | Adds major simulation complexity without necessary gameplay value. |
| Gravity generated by ships/asteroids/projectiles | Rejected | Unnecessary complexity and negligible intended player value. |
| Exact module-position mass distribution | Rejected for current scope | Total mass should matter, but detailed center-of-mass shifts and per-module inertia distribution are not currently worth the complexity. |
| Physical collision with celestial bodies | Rejected | Celestial bodies are visually represented on a deeper parallax layer and are not same-plane collision geometry. |
| Physical collision with stations | Rejected | Stations are prescribed non-collision world objects under the current presentation model. |
| Weapon recoil | Rejected | Adds control disturbance without enough gameplay value for the intended combat model. |
| Gameplay fuel/reaction-mass consumption for normal thrust | Rejected for current scope | Would make fuel logistics dominate movement before that gameplay has been intentionally designed. |
| Crew biological acceleration limits | Deferred | Could be reconsidered only if a later crew/life-support design gives it meaningful gameplay value. |

## Open Questions

No implementation-blocking Flight Physics gameplay questions remain at this stage.

The following details are intentionally owned by later concepts rather than unresolved here:

- exact structural-damage response to overload;
- exact Flight Assist behaviour;
- exact autopilot trajectory-planning behaviour;
- exact ship/module/cargo masses and thrust values;
- exact projectile and weapon parameters;
- exact authored/procedural orbital parameters for celestial bodies and stations.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A dynamic ship continues moving at constant velocity when no net force acts on it.
- [ ] A rotating ship continues rotating when no net torque acts on it.
- [ ] Increasing total ship mass while keeping thrust unchanged reduces linear acceleration.
- [ ] Additional ship mass increases rotational inertia without requiring detailed module-position simulation.
- [ ] Forward thrust capability is stronger than reverse and lateral thrust for ships following the baseline design.
- [ ] No hidden linear-speed or angular-speed clamp is required by the gameplay concept.
- [ ] Only celestial bodies generate gravity, while ships, projectiles, asteroids and debris do not.
- [ ] A dynamic object's gravitational acceleration does not depend on its own mass.
- [ ] Multiple celestial gravitational influences combine simultaneously and have no hard range cutoff.
- [ ] Per-body gravity softening prevents singular acceleration near a celestial center.
- [ ] Celestial bodies and stations can move along prescribed trajectories without being dynamically displaced by gravity or impacts.
- [ ] Celestial bodies and stations do not produce collision responses when their projected visual areas are crossed.
- [ ] Interactive asteroids respond to gravity and dynamic-object collisions.
- [ ] Dynamic-object collisions can change both linear and angular motion and preserve momentum at the gameplay level.
- [ ] Collision severity scales with relative motion and mass rather than a fixed per-object damage number.
- [ ] Projectiles inherit the firing object's velocity plus their own launch velocity.
- [ ] Firing a weapon does not recoil the firing ship.
- [ ] Detached debris inherits physically consistent movement from its source object.
- [ ] Excessive linear/angular loading can exceed ship-specific structural limits and be exposed to the later damage/structure system.
- [ ] Player and NPC ships obey the same flight-physics rules.
- [ ] All authoritative velocities and momenta can be interpreted in one global inertial reference frame.
